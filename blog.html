RF Engineering Field Notes: Planning and Troubleshooting Wireless Links

In my role as an Assistant Network Engineer on the RF team at Amber IT, I supported wireless internet service provider (WISP) infrastructure through RF planning, link budgeting, spectrum analysis, site surveys, and troubleshooting. The work involved exposure to platforms from Cambium Networks, Ubiquiti, and Mimosa in field deployment and maintenance contexts.

Practical considerations in wireless deployment

Wireless link quality depends on the radio platform, frequency, channel conditions, antenna configuration, installation quality, and the surrounding RF environment. These factors need to be considered together rather than relying on a device's headline throughput specification.

Line of sight and link budget

Site surveys and link-budget checks help assess path feasibility before deployment. Antenna alignment, path clearance, cable and connector losses, and expected fade margin all affect link reliability.

Spectrum conditions

Spectrum analysis helps identify noise and potential interference in the local environment. Channel selection and channel width should be considered alongside regulatory requirements, device capabilities, and the needs of nearby links.

Point-to-point and point-to-multipoint links

Point-to-point links connect two network locations, while point-to-multipoint deployments serve multiple remote stations from a common access point. The planning and troubleshooting approach differs, particularly around alignment, airtime, capacity, and interference.

My Role: RF & Wireless Network Support

During my role as an Assistant Network Engineer in the RF team at Amber IT, I supported wireless access and backhaul activities, including:

  • Assisting with RF planning, link budgeting, and frequency review.
  • Conducting site surveys and line-of-sight checks.
  • Using spectrum analysis to investigate interference and channel conditions.
  • Supporting radio and antenna installation, alignment, testing, and troubleshooting.
  • Coordinating with NOC teams during BTS, RF, and LAN incidents, outages, and maintenance windows.
  • Validating basic Layer 2/Layer 3 items, including VLANs, trunks, and basic routing where required.

Platforms encountered

Equipment in the environments I supported included:

  • Cambium Networks ePMP: ePMP Force 180, 190, 200, and related platform deployments.
  • Mimosa: C5x wireless radios.
  • Ubiquiti: PowerBeam M5 radios.

Exact performance and frequency specifications vary by model, firmware, antenna configuration, and regulatory region. Refer to the relevant vendor documentation for device-specific figures.
